# Test run: mutual fund
Date: 10 Nov 2026
First prompt: Should I put my bonus of 15000 into the Aditya Birla Sun Life Mutual Fund?

# response:

Before I can say anything useful, I need to know which fund you mean. Aditya Birla Sun Life is a fund house that runs dozens of schemes, from liquid and debt funds to large-cap, flexi-cap, small-cap and sectoral equity funds. They carry very different risks, so “should I invest in ABSL” has no single answer. A liquid fund and a small-cap fund are nearly different products.

Since ₹15,000 is a modest amount, I’ll run a quick version of a pre-mortem check (what could go wrong, and what you’d do about it) rather than a full one. I need a few details first:

Which scheme exactly, and is it a lump sum or a monthly SIP?
What is this ₹15,000 relative to your savings? For example, is it 5% or 50% of what you have invested or saved?
When might you need this money? If it could be within 1 to 2 years, that changes which fund types make sense.
Where did the idea come from? Your own research, a friend, a bank or agent, or an ad? Is there any deadline attached (“NFO closing”, “offer ends”)?
Do you already have an emergency fund (roughly 3 to 6 months of expenses) set aside separately?
A general point while you think about it: if the idea came through a bank relationship manager or agent, check whether you’d be buying a regular plan or a direct plan. Regular plans carry a higher expense ratio because they include distributor commission, and over many years that can add up. Check the current expense ratio and exit load on the scheme’s factsheet or the AMC’s website before committing.

Send me those answers and I’ll walk through the main ways it could go wrong, how much of the pitch is checkable fact versus story, and whether the size fits your situation.

Not financial advice. This is a decision-discipline exercise, not a recommendation. Consider a licensed advisor for your situation.
