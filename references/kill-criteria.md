# Writing kill criteria

Kill criteria are rules made **before** buying, when you are calm, to be followed **after**, when you are not.

## Properties of a good rule
- **Testable**: a stranger could look at the facts and say whether it triggered.
- **Specific**: numbers, dates, named events.
- **Independent of mood**: "if I feel scared" is not a rule.
- **Tied to the thesis**: if the reason you bought breaks, you leave, regardless of price.
- **Pre-committed and confirmed** by the user in their own words.

## Required set (4-6 rules)

1. **Re-buy test**: *"I sell if I would no longer buy this today, at today's price, with fresh money."*
2. **Thesis-break**: *"I sell if [Step 1 leading indicator] happens."*
3. **Loss checkpoint**: *"At -X% I stop and re-run the pre-mortem. I don't average down until I've done that."* (A checkpoint forces a review. It is not an automatic sale unless the user chooses that.)
4. **Time rule**: *"If I can't re-justify this at the review date, I treat it as a default hold and reduce or exit."*
5. **Size rule**: *"If this grows past Y% of my portfolio, I trim to Z%."*
6. **Behaviour rule**: *"I won't add money, borrow to hold, or change these rules without writing down why."*

## Anti-patterns to flag
- "I'll sell when it recovers." (anchoring to purchase price)
- "I'll decide when it happens." (no rule)
- Rules only about price, none about thesis.
- Stop-losses on illiquid assets that can't execute.
- Rules the user hasn't confirmed.
