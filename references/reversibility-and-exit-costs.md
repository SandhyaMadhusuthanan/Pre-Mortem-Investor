# Reversibility score and exit-cost map

## Score

| Score | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two-way door. Undo in a day at negligible cost. | Liquid large-cap stock or broad ETF, small amount |
| 2 | Mostly reversible. Small friction. | Open-ended mutual fund with small exit load |
| 3 | Reversible with a cost. | Fixed deposit with premature-withdrawal penalty; small-cap with wide spread |
| 4 | Hard to reverse. Large cost or long delay. | Locked-in products, thinly traded assets, private placements |
| 5 | One-way door. Effectively irreversible. | Property purchase, off-plan deposits, long-tenure loans with heavy prepayment penalties, illiquid private deals, many early-stage tokens |

Modifiers: add 1 for leverage, add 1 if the exit depends on a counterparty's cooperation, subtract 1 for a regulated, daily-liquidity market. Cap at 1-5.

## Questions to ask
- What is the cheapest way out and the most likely way out? They're often different.
- Is there a cooling-off or free-look period (common for insurance, some loans and some products)? When does it end?
- What happens if the platform, developer, or lender fails?
- Can part of the position be exited, or is it all-or-nothing?
